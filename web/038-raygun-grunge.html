CHLORINE

PONY

we always never meant to be loud.Chlorine Pony drained a swimming pool, filled it with feedback, and called it a hymnal. Words by R. Okafor. Pictures: whatever the photocopier felt like.

Chlorine Pony are three people and a drum machine named Gerald, from an Ohio town with two stoplights, one of which has been blinking yellow since 1987. Their fourth record, Swimming Pool Hymns, was recorded in a drained municipal pool in eleven days, and it sounds exactly like that: wet, tiled, echoing and slightly dangerous. We met them in a laundromat off Route 9 because, in Dana's words, it is “the only room in town with honest reverb.” Marco brought a bag of quarters. Nobody washed anything.

Your last album was called Quiet Please. This one is extremely loud. What happened?

DANA“Quiet Please” was a request, not a title. Nobody listened. So we stopped listening too.

You recorded in an empty swimming pool.

MARCOThe deep end has a natural reverb of 4.2 seconds. The shallow end is where we kept the snacks. You have to respect the zoning.

Who, exactly, is Gerald?

DANAA Roland drum machine we bought at a garage sale for six dollars. He is the only member who has never been late, never been drunk, and never asked for a solo.

IT'S NOT NOISE IF YOU MEAN IT.— DANA, BETWEEN SPIN CYCLES

Do you worry about selling out?

MARCO — We sold out once. A car commercial wanted “Lifeguard on Fire.” Then they heard it. We still have the fax where they said no.

What's next?

DANA — A tour of every drained pool in the Midwest. Bring a towel. Not for swimming. For crying.

Last words?

GERALD — [four minutes of hi-hat]

Theo, you've been quiet all afternoon.

THEO — Well, I think of the bass as the floor of the song, and nobody ever thanks the floor, you know, you walk on it all day and you never once say “hey floor, great job,” and that's fine, that's the job, but when we were in the pool I started thinking about how the pool is also a floor, like, a floor with walls that's waiting to become a lake, and the bass is kind of like the water that isn't there, and when you play in an empty pool you're basically playing the absence of water, which I think is what the whole record is about, the absence of water, or maybe the absence of Gerald's feelings, because he's a drum machine, and I wrote a song about that but they said it was nine minutes too long, which, fair, but also, a floor is as long as the room.

THEO'S ANSWER RAN FOUR MINUTES. WE SET IT IN 5 POINT. YOU'RE WELCOME. — ED. (click it, if you must)
