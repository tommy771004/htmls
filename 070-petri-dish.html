A living culture of Conwayella vulgaris, stained H&E

Specimen No. 70 · incubated on a 160 × 160 lattice · Petri radius 78 cells

Abstract. Each nucleus lives, dies or is born according to its eight neighbours and nothing else. With three neighbours, life appears; with two or three, it persists; otherwise it succumbs to loneliness or crowding — a finding the authors describe as “relatable”. Newborn cells stain dark with haematoxylin; dying ones fade to eosin pink.
